# Recommended resources

These readings and references support the *Synthesis of the Archazolid Natural
Products* project. They cover the background on the archazolids and V-ATPase,
prior fragment syntheses (especially from the O'Neil group), and the two core
techniques you will use: air-free Schlenk work and NMR characterization. Your
PI will point you to the specific fragment and the most relevant primary
papers — treat this list as a starting orientation, not a complete syllabus.

## Background: the archazolids and why they matter

- **Review — Total syntheses of the archazolids: an emerging class of novel
  anticancer drugs**, *Beilstein Journal of Organic Chemistry* (2017),
  [open access](https://www.beilstein-journals.org/bjoc/articles/13/108). The
  best single overview of the molecules, their V-ATPase activity, and the
  various synthetic strategies (including the O'Neil group's work). Read this
  first for the big picture.
- Background on **V-ATPase** as a cancer target — any recent review on
  vacuolar-type ATPase in tumor biology will explain why an inhibitor like the
  archazolids is of interest.

## Prior fragment syntheses (O'Neil group)

These show what a real fragment route looks like and what NMR evidence is used
to confirm structure and stereochemistry. They are the closest models for your
own work.

- **A Synthesis of the C3–C15 Fragment of the Archazolids**, O'Neil & Black,
  *Synlett*,
  [abstract](https://www.thieme-connect.com/products/ejournals/abstract/10.1055/s-0029-1218537).
  Note how ring-closing metathesis and an allylation–elimination sequence build
  the conjugated triene, and how stereochemistry is confirmed by NMR (see the
  Supporting Information).
- **Synthesis of the C1–C23 Fragment of the Archazolids** (convergent Stille
  coupling to build the sensitive Z,Z,E-triene) — summarized in this
  [SYNFORM highlight](https://www.thieme.de/en/thieme-chemistry/towards-the-c1-c23-fragment-of-the-archazolids-116433.htm).
- **Exploring a Ring-Closing Metathesis Approach to the Archazolid Macrocycle**,
  [open access via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6754729/). A
  good example of fragment chemistry toward a simplified dihydroarchazolid B
  analogue, including how a step that *failed* was diagnosed and rationalized.

## Technique: Schlenk line and air-free methods

- Any modern guide to **inert-atmosphere (Schlenk) technique** — degassing
  solvents, working under N₂/Ar, and syringe/cannula transfer of
  air-sensitive reagents. Your group will have a preferred reference and an
  in-person checkout; use both.
- A standard advanced organic lab text covering **handling air- and
  moisture-sensitive reagents** is a useful companion at the bench.

## Technique: NMR for reaction monitoring and characterization

- Your introductory organic chemistry **spectroscopy chapter** is enough to
  start reading ¹H NMR; revisit chemical shift, integration, and coupling.
- For confirming stereochemistry and full structures you will likely use **2D
  NMR** (COSY, HSQC, NOESY). Any graduate-level NMR primer or your
  instrument's documentation will cover how to acquire and read these.

## A note on scope

You are reproducing and characterizing **one fragment**, not completing the
natural product. The literature above describes work by experienced groups over
many years — use it to understand the strategy and the techniques, and lean on
your PI to set a realistic target for a single project.
